# Reply Handling & Follow-Up Spine

> **One-line promise:** every reply classified, answered on time, and followed up three times — so the pipeline you paid to create never leaks at the last step.

| | |
|---|---|
| **OWNER** | Triage agent classifies; you approve and send (twice-daily reply sweep) |
| **TRIGGER** | Any inbound reply, on any channel |
| **THE NUMBER IT MOVES** | Sequence-to-meeting conversion (target: 4 meetings per 100 unique prospects touched) |
| **BELT** | Amber |

Replies are the most expensive object in your business — each one cost you list-building, copy, sends, and reputation. Handling them ad hoc is how operators lose deals they already earned.

## The six classifications (use exactly these)

| Class | Meaning | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| `positive` | Interest, a question, or a meeting signal | Respond within 4 working hours |
| `objection` | Specific pushback with a reason | Respond same day with the named objection addressed |
| `neutral` | "Who is this?" / "Send info" / unclear | Respond same day, one clarifying line + the resource |
| `negative` | Clear no, no reason | Mark, suppress from sequence, no response needed |
| `ooo` | Auto-reply, out of office | No action; sequence resumes after their return date |
| `unsubscribe` | Any opt-out language, however phrased | Suppress everywhere immediately. Non-negotiable. |

Six classes, frozen. Don't invent "warm-ish." If a reply doesn't fit, it's `neutral` and a human reads it.

## The System

1. **Every reply gets classified within minutes** of arrival — by your triage agent if you have one, by you on the reply sweep if you don't. The classification, a one-line summary, and the timestamp get written to the contact record automatically.
2. **The sequence stops the moment anyone replies.** Nothing torpedoes trust like a chirpy follow-up landing after a real conversation started. Reply = early exit, always.
3. **Positive replies get a human answer inside 4 working hours.** Speed is the single biggest controllable factor in reply-to-meeting conversion. Your agent can draft; you approve. The draft is never auto-sent.
4. **Objections get answered, then counted.** Each objection response addresses the stated reason and offers a smaller next step. Track objection volume as a leading indicator: objections trending up means your copy is hitting the wrong pain — that's a copy review, not a sales problem.
5. **Run the 3-touch follow-up on conversations that stall.** A positive reply that goes quiet gets: nudge at +3 days (restate the next step in one line) · value-add at +7 days (something useful, no ask) · soft close at +14 days ("happy to pick this up whenever — door's open"). Then stop. Three touches, then the lead goes to the reactivation pool (next page).
6. **Escalate decisions, not drafts.** The only replies that interrupt your day are ones requiring a real decision (pricing exception, scope question, complaint). Everything else waits for the sweep. If your median escalation-to-decision time passes 24 hours, *you* are the bottleneck — tighten the escalation criteria.
7. **Grade your agent weekly.** During the Friday review, spot-check classifications against your own judgment and log agree/disagree. Track agreement rate per class. An agent can graduate to auto-handling a class (start with `ooo` and `unsubscribe`) only after sustained ~90%+ agreement on a real sample. Trust is earned per-class, not granted globally.

## Copy This

The **Reply Log schema**: `timestamp · channel · contact · classification · confidence · one-line summary · your label (agree/disagree) · response sent at`. One row per reply. This log is simultaneously your QA on the agent, your copy-feedback loop, and your proof of response discipline when a prospect goes quiet and you want to know whose move it was.
