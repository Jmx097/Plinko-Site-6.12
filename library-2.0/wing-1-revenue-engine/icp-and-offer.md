# ICP & Offer

> **One-line promise:** a target definition sharp enough that a stranger could build your list, and an offer that earns replies instead of begging for meetings.

| | |
|---|---|
| **OWNER** | You (this is founder work — don't delegate it yet) |
| **TRIGGER** | Before any campaign launches; revisited quarterly |
| **THE NUMBER IT MOVES** | Positive reply rate (2%+ is the bar for a sustainable cold motion) |
| **BELT** | Amber |

## The System

1. **Write the firmographic box.** Industry, headcount band, geography, seniority of buyer. Be narrow: a band like "11–50 employees, US/Canada, owner or ops leader" beats "SMBs" every time. Use the **NicheSnap ICP Brief Builder** (Vault) to force the questions you'd rather skip.
2. **Add the signal layer.** Firmographics alone are a list, not a target. A signal is a *change or event* that makes this the right month to show up. The ones that convert, in priority order: a new leader hired into the role that buys what you sell (budget + mandate converge in their first 90 days); an active job posting whose description names the pain you solve (they wrote your opener for you); headcount growth of 10–30% in a year (ops stretched, not yet broken); recent funding or acquisition (new money, new mandate); a buyer posting publicly about your problem space.
3. **Mark each signal P1, P2, or P3.** P1 = outreach within 72 hours of detection. P2 = work into the weekly cadence. P3 = ambient, only acts as a tiebreaker. Volume is not the lever; signal precision is — well-timed sends convert at multiples of pure cold.
4. **Map the offer with the Offer Mapper** (Vault): the painful problem in their words, your mechanism, the proof you can show, the risk you remove.
5. **Choose a reply-first CTA.** The cold CTA is not "book a call" — it's "reply and I'll send you X," where X is a gated resource specific enough that they can see themselves in it. The strongest format: a short self-scoring diagnostic that ends with "reply with your score." It creates an open loop and qualifies them in one move.
6. **Write the one-sentence positioning test.** The first ten words of every email decide whether the next seventy get read. If you can't state the problem-POV in one sentence a buyer would nod at, go back to step 4.

## What does NOT count as a signal

"Matches my ICP" is not a signal. Neither is a profile view, a post like, or any metric without a date attached. Signals require something to have *changed*.
