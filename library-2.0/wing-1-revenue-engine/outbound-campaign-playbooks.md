# Outbound Campaign Playbooks

> **One-line promise:** the sequence structure, copy rules, and volume discipline that earn replies from cold strangers without burning your domain or your name.

| | |
|---|---|
| **OWNER** | You write and freeze the copy; the sending machine runs it |
| **TRIGGER** | Campaign launch (always a human decision), then the sequence runs itself |
| **THE NUMBER IT MOVES** | Reply rate (5%+ healthy; 8–15% top quartile) and positive reply rate (2%+ bar) |
| **BELT** | Amber |

## The System

1. **One campaign per segment.** Different buyers get different copy in different campaigns — never one blast with swapped tokens. An owner, a sales leader, and an ops leader read the same product through completely different pains. Unknown segment? Route to your default, but route consciously.
2. **Structure the sequence: 5 touches over ~17 days.** More isn't better — past 7 touches, each extra send risks more harm (unsubs, spam complaints) than lift. The shape that works: **T1** insight + gated-resource offer (most replies land here) · **T2** Day 4, a short "did this get buried?" bump under 40 words · **T3** Day 8, one anonymized proof metric + a pointed question about *their* business · **T4** Day 13, a one-line pattern interrupt (often the highest per-touch reply rate) · **T5** Day 17, a break-up with permission to close the loop.
3. **Anchor to the reply windows.** Mid-week mornings (local time) win. Put your strongest touches on Wednesday/Friday mornings; send the break-up early in the week so replies land before Friday.
4. **Subject lines: 36–50 characters, lowercase, specific.** Reference a real problem or outcome, not your offer. Banned: "Quick question," "Partnership opportunity," anything starting "I hope this finds you well" — spam-filter magnets and reply killers.
5. **Write in the copy style that passes the human sniff test.** No em dashes (the #1 AI tell). "Hey Name," with a comma. Founder voice: "I," not "we." Peer texting a peer, not a vendor drafting an email. Every word you'd cut from a text message, cut here.
6. **Personalize with tokens you can trust.** First name + company name, with a cleaner that strips ", Inc." suffixes and a fallback for blanks (a merge token rendering as empty mid-sentence kills the send). Per-lead custom openers are a separate, deliberate upgrade — don't bolt them onto frozen copy.
7. **Run dual-channel as one conversation.** Email sequence + LinkedIn touches (profile view → connect with a short note → message) on the same lead, checked for conflicts before launch so nobody gets the same pitch twice in one morning. Small universe? Go LinkedIn-first.
8. **Respect the volume constraint.** Your daily send cap (set by mailbox count and warmup status) is not a growth lever — it's a discipline that forces signal precision. At a fixed cap you'll cycle your whole addressable list in months; burning it twice as fast with worse targeting is how lists die. 1–2 contacts per company converts at roughly double the rate of contact-flooding.
9. **Freeze the copy for the test window.** Pick a window (we use 90 days), write the copy, and don't retune mid-window — otherwise you'll never know what worked. Log every "I should tweak this" urge for the next window.

## Signal-triggered overlays

The sequence above is the cold baseline. When a P1 signal fires (new ops hire, job posting in your pain vocabulary — see ICP & Offer), that lead skips the queue and gets a named-trigger opener within 72 hours: "Saw you just stepped into the ops lead role at [Co] — the first 90 days is usually where X surfaces." Signal-triggered sends convert at multiples of cold; they're worth interrupting the cadence for. Track them separately so you can prove it.

## Agents Attached

The campaign loader (Wing 3) fills paused campaigns. The reply-triage agent (next page) catches everything that comes back. A first-line personalization agent can draft openers, but every send still launches behind your gate.

## Copy This

The **subject-line starter set** (adapt the variables to your ICP): `a thought on {{company}}'s [function]` · `{{first_name}}, quick [niche] question` · `what 40 [niche] owners told us about [pain]` · `are you still [painful task] at 9pm?` · `the $[X]k mistake 3 [niche]s made last quarter`. Test 2–3 per persona track; keep the winners, kill the rest at the window boundary.
